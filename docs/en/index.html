01The problem we work on

Technology lets us see them.
It hasn’t solved companionship.

Five moments happen every day. The first four work. After the fifth, the connection stops.

  1. 01 You leave home Work / business trip / travel
  2. 02 Missing and worrying “What are they doing right now?”
  3. 03 You open the camera Actively looking for them
  4. 04 One-way interaction Watch / talk / feed
  5. 05 Disconnected again The device closes; the relationship pauses

The break point

Every connection has to begin with you remembering them.
They have no way to reach you first.

02What exists today

See them, talk to them, feed them.
Then what?

We looked at five representative products, from $56 to $199. They do monitoring and play well, and then stop in the same place.

What they already do

  • Show you what your pet is doing
  • Let them hear your voice
  • Feed or play with them remotely
  • Reassure you for a moment

What’s still missing

  • Interaction is almost always owner-initiated
  • Your pet can’t reach you first
  • Connection ends when the screen closes
  • Emotional support stays fragmented

03Who we design for

Not every pet owner.

But those who are deeply bonded with their pet and are often unable to be in the same space.

  • Urban working ownersAway from home for long hours
  • Frequent business or short-trip travellersRegular separation
  • People living alone with a strong bondTheir pet is an important source of company

What they share: their pet is no longer just a pet — it is family. Yet everyday life means they can’t always share the same space.

04The opportunity

From remote monitoring
to remote companionship.

TodayRemote Monitoring

  • See what the pet is doing
  • Owner talks to the pet
  • Owner feeds or plays remotely
  • Interaction ends and disconnects

The opportunityRemote Companionship

Connection can start from either side.

  • Let them reach out tooA way in that starts from the pet’s own natural behaviour
  • Make missing them tangibleTurn “I miss them” into a real interaction
  • No need to stay onlineMeaningful response without watching a screen
  • From moments to a relationshipCompanionship that doesn’t end when the screen closes

06Design principles

The question we’re answering

When an owner can’t be with their pet,
how can the emotional connection between them
still happen naturally?

  • Informed明察Know they’re okay, simply and reassuringly
  • Connected相连Feel a meaningful two-way bond, even apart
  • Respectful尊重Follow their nature; don’t disturb their rest
  • Supportive支持Fit into your life without adding weight

07What we’re testing

Four things we’re exploring.

  1. 01They can start it tooA way in that the pet can trigger through natural behaviour
  2. 02A light two-way responseNo constant screen time, no frequent interruption for them
  3. 03Keep the shared everydayNot only live video, but moments you can come back to
  4. 04A relationship that lastsFrom a single interaction toward ongoing connection

Where we are

Research, competitor analysis and design principles — no product yet. Next: interview owners, build prototypes, and observe whether people and pets actually choose to use them.

A few things you might wonder

How is this different from a pet camera?

Cameras mainly help you check in, talk and offer treats. We’re looking at three other things: whether a pet can initiate a connection, how you could respond when you’re offline, and how connection continues after a single interaction. All of it still needs research and prototype testing.

Can I buy or use something yet?

No. We’re at the research and concept stage, and there is no finished product. If prototype testing opens up, we’ll let subscribers know by email.
