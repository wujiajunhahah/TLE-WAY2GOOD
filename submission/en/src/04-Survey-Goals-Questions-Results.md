## 2. Survey Goals (3 core goals)

**Goal 1 Confirm whether "human–pet separation" is a high-frequency routine or an occasional event**
Turn "separation" from a vague feeling into a quantifiable frequency distribution, and judge whether this is a daily pain point worth building a product for.

**Goal 2 Measure the coverage and satisfaction of existing care methods, and locate which link has the gap**
Existing solutions fall into three categories: relying on other people, ordinary cameras, and smart pet devices. What needs to be measured is not merely "whether it is used" but "whether it is enough" — including satisfaction on two distinct dimensions: "knowing the state" and "feeling connected".

**Goal 3 Validate feature acceptance, the boundaries of automation and the price ceiling**
Against the three-stage capability of "monitoring — understanding — interaction", measure which stage users consider most valuable, how far they accept automation, and where the price and psychological barriers lie.

> **The single most telling comparison in this survey**:

### Q11 · Concerns (multiple choice, maximum 2 options)

| Concern | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Disturbing or frightening the pet | 16 | 51.6% |
| Price or ongoing costs | 13 | 41.9% |
| Misjudging behaviour and sending inaccurate notifications | 9 | 29% |
| Privacy issues around filming or recording | 9 | 29% |
| The pet is not interested | 7 | 22.6% |
| Device operating safety issues | 2 | 6.5% |
| Notifications make me more distracted or burdened | 1 | 3.2% |

> **The leading concern is not price but "disturbing or frightening the pet" (51.6%).** What users really want to know is: "Will it frighten my cat?" "Will it judge things wrongly?" "Is it safe to leave it on all the time?"

### Q12 · Highest level of automatic action allowed (n = 31)

| Option | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Playing a recording or starting a toy within set periods and durations | 12 | 38.7% |
| The system choosing the timing and manner of interaction within set limits | 10 | 32.3% |
| Record or notify only, without making sounds or movements towards the pet | 7 | 22.6% |
| No automatic action or notification allowed | 2 | 6.5% |

**Accepting some degree of automatic interaction = 12 + 10 = 22 (71%).**
**No automatic action at all allowed: only 2 (6.5%).**

> Design implication: **bounded automation / human-in-the-loop**, rather than fully autonomous AI. What users want is "I set the boundaries, the system acts within them".

### Q13 · One-time device price ceiling (n = 31)

| Price band | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| CNY 300–599 | 14 | 45.2% |
| CNY 1–299 | 7 | 22.6% |
| CNY 600–999 | 7 | 22.6% |
| CNY 1,000–1,499 | 3 | 9.7% |
| CNY 1,500 or more | 0 | 0% |

**At least CNY 300 acceptable: 24 (77.4%).**
**Above CNY 600 acceptable: 10 (32.3%).**

> The price sweet spot is **CNY 300–599** (45.2%). At the same time 41.9% list "price or ongoing costs" as a leading concern → the business model should not start with a heavy subscription; the recommendation is: **affordable hardware + free basic features + optional AI / cloud service subscription**.

## 5. Key Conclusions

1. **Separation is a high-frequency routine, not an occasional travel need**: 74.2% are separated for more than 4 consecutive hours on at least 3 days a week.
2. **Existing solutions depend on people, and are only "adequate"**: 64.5% rely on family members or carers; the two satisfaction dimensions reach only 32.3% and 38.7% respectively.
3. **Real-time video is a baseline expectation, not a differentiator**: 90.3% consider it valuable, but when "only one may be chosen" only 29% do so.
4. **The real opportunity lies in "controlled automatic companionship"**: 71% accept some form of automatic interaction, and what they want is "I set the boundaries, the system acts within them".
5. **The leading concern is the pet's experience, not price**: 51.6% worry about disturbing or frightening the pet, higher than price (41.9%).
6. **The price sweet spot is CNY 300–599**: 45.2% fall within this band.

### One-sentence version

> **Users do not simply want to watch their pets remotely — they want to remain connected with them.**

## 6. Potential Biases and Limitations

| # | Bias | Explanation | Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sample size still small** | 31 responses, only just reaching the lower limit required by the course | Percentages are unstable; 1 person = 3.2% |
| 2 | **Sample highly homogeneous** | 93.5% mainland China, 93.5% office workers or students, 64.5% cat owners | Cannot be extrapolated to all pet owners |
| 3 | **Self-reported preference, not real behaviour** | Q10 measures "willingness to use when price is ignored" | 77.4% is stated intention, not equivalent to a real purchase rate |
| 4 | **Convenience sampling** | Distribution channels and sampling method were not recorded | Possible clustering within the same circle |
| 5 | **Price question separated from willingness question** | Q10 explicitly says "ignoring price", Q13 asks about price separately | The two cannot be multiplied to derive willingness to pay |
| 6 | **Questionnaire design flaws** | Q9 contains two options labelled "D"; Q4 has an unnamed placeholder option | Does not affect the statistics for individual options, but reflects insufficient checking before distribution |
| 7 | **No open-ended questions** | All 13 questions are closed-ended | Cannot capture users' own language or issues not anticipated by the researchers |

### Wording discipline

One may say:

> "Among our surveyed respondents…" / "Our preliminary survey suggests…"

One may not say:

> "74.2% of all pet owners want our product."

**Positioning**: Preliminary Quantitative Validation, not large-scale market research.
