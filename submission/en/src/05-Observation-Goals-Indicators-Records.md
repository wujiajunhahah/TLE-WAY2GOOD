## 2. Observation Goals

**Goal 1 · Record what the pet actually does when no one is present**
Interviews can only capture "what the owner thinks the cat is doing", whereas observation captures "what the cat is actually doing". The gap between the two is where the opportunity for this project lies.

**Goal 2 · Record who initiates owner–pet interactions**
Record specifically: **who moves first** — does the person walk towards the cat, or the cat towards the person? How long does the interaction last? Who ends it first?

**Goal 3 · Record interference from the environment with the pet's rest**
Device location, noise level, foot traffic, and the number of times the pet is interrupted and how it reacts.

**Goal 4 · Record the actual device placement and blind spots of existing pet-care devices**
Where the camera is installed, what it can capture, and **what it cannot capture**.

## 3. Behavioural Indicators Table (the core of Field Note Preparation)

> This section constitutes the behavioural indicators (indicators of behavior) required by the course. **Only countable content is recorded in the field**; interpretation is written in a separate column.

### A. Pet side (cat)

| Indicator | How to record | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of approaches initiated by the pet towards people** | count + time point + target | directly tests "who initiates the interaction" |
| **Who ends the interaction** | cat walks away / person walks away / interrupted | assesses interaction quality |
| scratching behaviour | object (sofa / cat tree / carpet), duration in seconds, number of times | determines whether it is a claw-sharpening need or a location preference |
| resting location | specific location + whether it was moved | environmental security |
| **Number of interruptions** | source of each interruption (noise / person / other animal) | rest quality |
| reaction to sudden sounds | ears pricked / motionless / hiding / no reaction | assesses the risk of adding sound to a new device |
| distance kept from people | approaches of its own accord / stays more than 1 m away / avoids | degree of closeness |
| multi-cat households: distance and interaction between the two cats | together / apart / conflict | resource allocation |

### B. Owner side (person)

| Indicator | How to record |
|---|---|
| **Number and duration of glances at the phone / monitor** | count + seconds each time (this item yields the richest material) |
| number and duration of interactions initiated with the pet | count + seconds |
| after being interrupted by work or others, **whether they return** to the pet | yes / no |
| topics raised when mentioning the pet | verbatim fragments |
| explanations of the pet's behaviour | verbatim (note: record separately from the observed facts) |

### C. Environment side

| Indicator | How to record |
|---|---|
| noise level | description + subjective rating (quiet / moderate / loud) |
| foot traffic | number of people passing per unit of time |
| **device placement** | photographs + sketch of the positions |
| **blind spot** | explicitly state the places the camera cannot capture |
| accessible area for the pet | where it can go, where it cannot go, what blocks it |

### Setting 2 · Pet owner's home (a setting where the pet is alone) **To be done (about 40 minutes)**

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Planned time | a different date from Setting 1 |
| Place | any interviewee (Christian's / Randy's / James's home, or your own home if you keep a cat) |
| People | pet-keeping households where the resident lives alone or no one is at home during the day |

### Setting 3 · Public setting (a pet in an unfamiliar environment) **To be done (about 30 minutes)**

| Item | Content |
|---|---|
| Planned place | veterinary clinic waiting area / pet shop / dog-walking area in the residential compound / stray-cat feeding point (choose one) |
| People | owners taking their pets out (**a completely different group** from the previous two settings) |
| Goal | record the pet's behaviour in an unfamiliar environment and how owners soothe and manage it |

## 6. 10 Insights for Further Investigation

> The slides explicitly require **10**. The following are **leads for further investigation** based on the existing material (not conclusions).

| # | Insight (lead to be checked) | Source | How to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The cat's rest **is already being interrupted by the environment**, yet the owner is aware of it and has not addressed it | Setting 1 | follow up on the music volume and the cat's sleeping hours |
| 2 | Scratching the sofa despite the presence of a cat tree → possibly not "nothing to scratch" but a **location or material preference** | Setting 1 | record the distance between the scratching location and the cat tree |
| 3 | The cat **does approach people of its own accord** → interactions are not all initiated by people | interview + observation | count: the number of approaches initiated by the pet per unit of time |
| 4 | The owner's explanations of the cat's behaviour ("tamed by customers", "does not need play time") are **inconsistent** with observable behaviour | Setting 1 | record "facts" and the "owner's explanation" separately for the same behaviour |
| 5 | The camera serves both **security** and **cat-watching** purposes, yet the owner mentions only one of them | two interviews | follow up on "what the camera was originally installed for" |
| 6 | The care chain relies on being "**on the way**" (the worker feeds the cat on his way past), which is free-riding rather than commitment | Interview 5 | follow up on who is responsible on days when he does not go, and whether any problems have occurred |
| 7 | The emotionally invested person and the actual carer **are not the same person** | Interview 5 | follow up on the division of daily feeding / cleaning / emergency handling |
| 8 | If a new device **emits sound**, it is added on top of an already noisy environment rather than starting from zero | Setting 1 + survey 51.6% | observe the pet's reaction to sudden sounds |
| 9 | The owner's **frequency of checking the monitor** may be far higher than the number of times he states | to be tested | timed recording of the number of "glances at the phone" in Settings 1/2 |
| 10 | The intensity of the cat's stress response in an **unfamiliar environment** (clinic / pet shop) may determine the form of the device | Setting 3 | observe and record a grading of stress responses |
