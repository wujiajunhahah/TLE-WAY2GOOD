---

## 1. Interview Goals

The interview is not intended to answer "do users like this product", but three more fundamental questions.

**Goal 1: Clarify what "separation" looks like in real life**
Not "would you miss your pet", but: which parts of the day are you away, where is the pet at that time, who is on site, and what did you do. Turn "separation" from a feeling into a schedule.

**Goal 2: Identify "the specific moment at which current methods fall short"**
All participants use some means of caring for their pet — family members, a camera, delegated care. What the interview must locate is not an overall judgement of whether it is "good enough", but the specific moment at which it fails.

**Goal 3: Distinguish "what the owner says" from "what the owner does"**
Pet owners' explanations of their own care practices are often a self-consistent account. The interview must record both their account and observable behaviour; it is where the two are inconsistent that the product opportunity lies.

## 2. Interview Hypotheses

| No. | Hypothesis | Criterion (what counts as refuted) |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Separation from the pet is a high-frequency everyday event, rather than an occasional business trip or travel | Refuted if most participants are separated from their pet for fewer than 3 days per month |
| H2 | Existing solutions (family members + camera) provide visibility, not response capability | Refuted if participants are able to act on what they see in a timely manner |
| H3 | Owners accept automation, but the boundaries must be set by themselves; and the device must not disturb the pet | Refuted if participants are willing to leave decisions entirely to the device, or do not mind the device making a sound |

**H2 was refined after the on-site interviews**: the gap is not in "seeing", but in "who can respond after seeing". See the interview results document for details.

## 3. The 10 Prepared Questions

The following is the outline actually used in the interviews, divided into five groups by module.

| No. | Question | Module |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Could you briefly introduce the story of you and your pet? (breed, age, and the role it plays in your life) | Emotional connection |
| 2 | When you come home from work or see it at the end of a tiring day, what is the greatest emotional comfort it brings you? | Emotional connection |
| 3 | When you have to leave home (such as for work, a business trip or travel), what mood or worries do you usually have? | Separation anxiety |
| 4 | During the time when you cannot be with it during the day, do you miss it frequently? What means do you usually use to relieve this "separation anxiety"? | Separation anxiety |
| 5 | What means do you currently use (such as a smart camera, care by family members, boarding at a pet shop) to maintain contact with it? | Current methods |
| 6 | When using your current device, do you feel that it can genuinely relieve your anxiety? Or does it sometimes make you more worried instead? | Current methods |
| 7 | In the process of "seeing" your pet remotely, which moments give rise to a sense of emotional loss? | Current methods |
| 8 | If there were a solution that helped you build a deeper emotional connection with your pet, what functions would you most want it to have? | Concept validation |
| 9 | Compared with a "cold data report", would you prefer to receive "warm emotional feedback"? Why? | Concept validation |
| 10 | What kind of remote companionship experience do you think would make you truly feel at ease and satisfied when you are away from home? | Concept validation |

For the on-site interviews (barber, farm pet owner), a further 10 questions focused on their life situation were prepared according to the same logic; see the interview record document.

## 4. List of Follow-up Questions

The following five follow-up chains were **reconstructed from the interview records**. Basis for reconstruction: the answer obtained by each follow-up is **verifiable in the original interview record**, and this information could not have come directly from the prepared questions (none of the 10 prepared questions covers it), so it must have arisen from a follow-up. The **wording of the follow-up questions** is reconstructed from context, not a verbatim record.

| No. | Participant | Prepared question | Initial answer | **Follow-up** | Answer obtained | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | I4 barber | Do you usually play with your cats? | Cats need no dedicated play; just put food down | **Have you ever bought them a toy?** | Bought several toys; the cats ignored them, so he saw no reason to buy more | Surfaces a **past attempt that failed** — the key to understanding why he believes none is needed, and an existing doubt the product must face |
| F2 | I4 barber | What are your daily hours like? | Arrives just after 1pm | **What time do you close? Do you do anything else after work?** | Works part-time at a bar until 5am | Establishes **cross-location separation at night** — determines whether this participant falls inside the target group |
| F3 | I4 barber | What are the two cats usually like? | The two cats feel very different | **Do you go to them, or do they come to you?** | The cats usually walk up to people on their own | Reveals that **the pet initiates approach** — directly relevant to the premise that the pet should be an active participant |
| F4 | I5 farm owner | Who mainly runs the farm? | His father runs it | **Who wanted the cat? Why keep it at the farm rather than at home?** | His father has little interest in pets; he himself wanted it; the family found keeping a cat at home troublesome, so it was placed at the farm | Reveals that **the person emotionally invested is not the person providing care**, and that the animal's location is shaped by family arrangements |
| F5 | I5 farm owner | What do you worry about regarding the cat? | He worries about the cat's safety | **What specifically are you worried about?** | There are many stray dogs near the farm, and another dog was previously killed | Grounds "worry" in a **specific risk object**; also confirms the incident involved a dog, not this cat |

### Basis for the reconstruction

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Verifiable element** | Every "answer obtained" comes directly from the interview record, with no addition or rewriting |
| **Reconstructed element** | The wording of the follow-up questions. The original record retained only the question-and-answer content, not the exact phrasing |
| **Boundary** | A follow-up is not listed here if no corresponding answer can be found in the record |

## 5. Discipline in Using Follow-up Questions

| Discipline | Description |
|---|---|
| Use only open-ended questioning | "Could you describe ...", "What were you thinking at the time ..." |
| Do not insert inferences into questions | Do not ask "do you feel guilty", "is the cat scratching the sofa because it is bored" |
| Distinguish spontaneous accounts from answers given after prompting | Mark the source when recording, to help judge the strength of the evidence later |
| Retain negative answers | "Not needed", "already good enough", "has not happened" are recorded as well |
| Record contradictions rather than smoothing them over | When a participant is inconsistent, ask about the reason, rather than keeping one version |
